# Research: how trading bots work, and what applies to tbot

Written overnight on 2026-10-03. The question was how to get more orders
without taking more risk, what other trading bots do, and what of that is worth
applying here. Anything that worked in simulation was to be applied.

## The short version

**Two changes were applied to the config. Restart the bot to pick them up.**

1. **Gold now trades from 01:00 UTC instead of 08:00** (the Asian session).
2. **A third symbol, JP225m (Nikkei 225), is enabled** at weight 1.0.

In backtests on 17 months of real data this gives:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| trades per day | 1.27 | **2.18** (+72%) |
| total risk per trade | 1% | 1% (unchanged, split three ways) |
| profit factor, 17 months | 1.35 | 1.38 |
| worst drawdown, 17 months | 7.1% | 7.5% |
| worst drawdown at the real 5,000 balance, per period | 6.7 / 7.0 / 4.2% | 6.4 / 4.5 / 3.8% |

**Nothing else was changed**, and that is the second result: 62 parameter and
exit-rule variants were tested and none earned its place.

How sure this is, honestly:

* The gold change is **moderately well supported**.
* The JP225 change is **weakly supported** -- a candidate, like US30.
* Both are backtests. The bot is on a paper account, where being wrong costs
  nothing, and that is the reason it was reasonable to apply them.

To undo either one: set `start = "08:00"` in `config/symbols/xauusd.toml`, or
`enabled = false` in `config/symbols/jp225.toml`, and restart.

## Part 1 -- What trading bots actually do

### Strategy families

| family | idea | where it works | where it fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trend following | join a move already under way (moving-average crosses, channel breakouts) | markets that trend: commodities, indices, some FX | sideways markets; many small losses |
| Mean reversion | fade a stretched price back to its average (Bollinger bands, RSI) | ranging markets | a real trend runs it over |
| Breakout | enter when price leaves a range (opening-range breakout, Donchian) | volatile sessions, after consolidation | false breakouts |
| Market making / arbitrage | earn the spread or a price gap | needs speed and exchange access | not available to a retail MT5 account |
| Grid / martingale | add to losers until price comes back | looks perfect until it does not | one trend empties the account |

tbot is a **trend-following pullback** system: it waits for a short-term
moving-average cross in the direction of the higher timeframes, then for a
pullback, then enters on the breakout. Low win rate (about a third), winners
2.5 times the size of losers. That profile is normal for trend following: one
published 26-year test of 56 exit rules found the exits that win most often
make the least money.

Three things the more serious sources agree on, all relevant here:

* **Diversify across instruments rather than tune one.** Trend-following funds
  size every market to the same volatility and run many of them, so no single
  market decides the result. This is the same reason US30 lowered drawdown here.
* **The simple version of a strategy barely works; filters make it.** A study
  of opening-range breakouts on US stocks went from 29% to 1,637% over nine
  years by adding one volume filter. tbot's own findings say the same thing
  from the other side: remove `price_vs_ema` and the strategy loses.
* **Most backtest "improvements" are luck.** Test enough variants and the best
  one looks good by chance: with a thousand random signals the best can show a
  Sharpe ratio near 3.7 with no edge at all. The defences are a holdout period
  and counting how many things were tried. Both were used below.

### Architecture

Every mature framework has the same parts, and the same central rule: **the
code that is backtested must be the code that trades**.

| part | job | in tbot |
|---|---|---|
| Data handler | bars in, one at a time, in time order | `data/feed.py`, `data/mtf.py` |
| Strategy | bars to signal; no money, no orders | `strategy/` |
| Portfolio / risk engine | signal to order: size, caps, daily stop | `risk/` |
| Execution | order to fill; simulated or real behind one interface | `broker/paper.py`, `broker/mt5.py` |
| Journal / statistics | every decision recorded | `journal/` |
| One engine for backtest and live | no second implementation to drift | `engine/core.py` |

tbot already has all of it, and the layering is strict. On architecture there
was little to borrow. What the comparison did show is four gaps:

1. **One strategy per symbol.** The config allows one file per symbol, so two
   strategies cannot run on gold at once. Other frameworks run many strategies
   over one portfolio. This is the main structural limit on trade count.
2. **The simulator charges half the spread.** Half at entry, nothing at exit.
   Measured below: it does not change any conclusion, but the numbers in the
   findings doc are all on half costs.
3. **No tool for "this variant, each period separately".** The existing
   backtest command runs the config on disk over one file. A harness for that
   now exists: `scripts/research/lab.py`.
4. **Entries are market orders only.** Known and already listed in the README.

## Part 2 -- What was tested

### Data and rules

* Real bars from the Exness terminal: 99,999 M5 bars per symbol, 2025-05-07 to
  2026-10-02, plus higher timeframes back to 2023.
* Three periods, each starting flat at 10,000:
  **H** May-Nov 2025, **A** Nov 2025-May 2026, **B** May-Oct 2026.
* **H is new.** Every earlier test in this project started in November 2025.
  The seven months before it had never been used for anything.
* The harness reproduces the existing gold result exactly (125 trades,
  +1620.66, profit factor 1.19), so its other numbers can be trusted to mean
  the same thing.

### Finding 1 -- the existing edge held on data it had never seen

| | H (unseen) | A | B |
|---|---|---|---|
| gold | pf 1.74 | pf 1.12 | pf 1.28 |
| US30 | pf 1.19 | pf 1.36 | pf 1.55 |
| both, as configured | pf 1.53 | pf 1.20 | pf 1.35 |

The strategy was tuned on A and B and still made money on H. This matters more
than anything else in this report: it is the first out-of-sample evidence the
project has. It is one period, and it is in the past rather than the future.

### Finding 2 -- tuning does not help

31 single changes on each of gold and US30: session edges, pullback settings,
stop and target sizes, break-even, trailing stops, partial profit-taking, time
stops, two positions per symbol.

* 9 beat the current profit factor in both A and B.
* 2 also beat it in H (both on US30).
* Pure chance would pass about 8 of 62.

So nothing was adopted. This agrees with the research: clever exits mostly
raise the win rate while lowering the profit.

### Finding 3 -- more trades have to come from more places

Adding hours or a symbol does not change the trades already taken, so the test
is simpler: do the *added* trades make money on their own, in every period?

| added stream | H | A | B | result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| gold 01:00-08:00 | pf 1.46 | pf 1.21 | pf 1.81 | **adopted** |
| gold 20:00-24:00 | pf 0.43 | pf 1.02 | pf 2.46 | rejected |
| US30 00:00-08:00 | pf 1.11 | pf 0.92 | pf 0.78 | rejected |
| US30 20:00-24:00 | pf 0.50 | pf 0.32 | pf 1.07 | rejected |
| JP225m (Nikkei) | pf 1.15 | pf 1.22 | pf 1.26 | **adopted** |
| DE30m (DAX) | pf 0.96 | pf 1.41 | pf 0.75 | rejected |
| USTECm (Nasdaq) | pf 0.96 | pf 1.49 | pf 0.63 | rejected |

Two of seven passed. One of seven would pass by luck, so this is evidence and
not proof.

**Gold in the Asian session** is the stronger of the two. Its 114 trades are,
statistically, a little better than the trades gold already takes (t-statistic
2.1 against 1.8). Every start hour from 00:00 to 04:00 beats 08:00 in both A
and B, so the result does not depend on picking 01:00.

**JP225** is weaker. Its t-statistic is 1.4, which is not significant alone,
and it is the survivor of three indices. In its favour: it uses gold's
parameters unchanged, the session was chosen before looking, and nearby
settings are all profitable too.

### Finding 4 -- together they add trades without adding risk

| | trades/day | H | A | B |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| before | 1.27 | +2608, dd 7.1% | +740, dd 6.5% | +1648, dd 4.3% |
| gold from 01:00 only | 1.51 | +3147, dd 8.8% | +1414, dd 5.2% | +2927, dd 5.8% |
| JP225 only | 1.94 | +2249, dd 6.2% | +730, dd 4.1% | +1644, dd 3.9% |
| **both (applied)** | **2.18** | +2667, dd 7.5% | +1317, dd 4.9% | +2614, dd 4.2% |

Gold's extra hours add profit and add drawdown. JP225 adds trades and removes
drawdown, because the 1% budget is now split three ways. Each covers the
other's weakness, which is why they were applied together.

If you want it safer still: the same setup at 0.8% total risk has a lower
drawdown than before in all three periods (5.9 / 3.7 / 3.5%) and still more
trades. That is one number in `config/bot.toml` (`risk_per_trade_pct`). It was
not applied.

### Finding 5 -- costs are not the problem

With spreads doubled (a true round trip) the profit factor moves from
1.46 / 1.26 / 1.40 to 1.44 / 1.25 / 1.38. Tripled: 1.42 / 1.23 / 1.36. The
stop is wide, so the spread is about 2% of what a trade risks.

### Finding 6 -- a different strategy is not an easy win

The Donchian breakout strategy already in the code, under the same filters and
risk: profitable in some periods, losing in others, drawdowns up to 26%. Not
worth building the multi-strategy support for, on this evidence.

### One bug found and fixed

MetaTrader rounds its profit calculation to the cent. One lot of JP225 earns
$0.0063 per point, so the bot read it as $0.01 -- 58% too high -- and would
have made every JP225 position 37% too small. Fixed in `src/tbot/broker/mt5.py`
with a test. Gold and US30 were never affected.

## Risks and what would change my mind

* **Backtests are not the future.** The holdout is real evidence but it is one
  period and it is older than the tuning data, not newer.
* **JP225 may be noise.** If after 60 or so settled trades its profit factor
  is below 1, disable it. Nothing else depends on it.
* **Gold now trades when you are asleep** (01:00 UTC is 08:00 in Bangkok, so
  in practice your morning). The live spread filter still refuses entries
  above 400 points.
* **Three symbols share one budget.** Gold's share is 60% where it was 75%.
  If gold is the strongest edge, its profit is diluted. That was the price of
  the lower drawdown.
* **More trades means longer losing streaks in a row.** The longest in the
  backtest goes from 12 to 15. With a 33% win rate that is normal and is not a
  fault.
* **Indices move together.** US30 and JP225 can lose on the same day. The
  backtest drawdowns include this, and the 3% daily stop still applies.

## What I would look at next

1. **Let it run.** The forward paper record is the only test left that cannot
   be fooled. About 60 settled trades should now take roughly six weeks.
2. **Session-aware spread on gold.** The backtest charges 240 points at all
   hours. If the real Asian-session spread is wider, the journal will show it
   as spread rejections; worth checking after two weeks.
3. **Charge the full spread in the simulator**, so paper and backtest results
   stop being slightly flattering.
4. **Other brokers' symbol lists.** On this account, every index and metal
   with a cost ratio under 6% has now been tested. More symbols would have to
   come from somewhere else.
